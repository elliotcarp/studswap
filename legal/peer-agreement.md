## About this document

This is an agreement **between two StudSwap users**, formed directly between them when both confirm an arrangement in the app.

**StudSwap is not a party to it.** StudSwap has no obligations under it, does not guarantee either party will perform it, does not handle the money that passes between the parties, and does not mediate or arbitrate disputes about it.

StudSwap provides this template as a convenience. The details below are filled in automatically from what both parties agreed in the app. Neither party is obliged to use this template, and both may agree different terms in writing — but StudSwap's own fees and cancellation rules (Section 8 of the [Terms of Service](/terms)) apply regardless, because those are between each user and StudSwap.

## 3. What each party promises about their accommodation

Each party providing accommodation confirms that:

**3.1 Right to offer it.** They have the legal right to occupy it for the dates listed, **and** their lease, housing contract, university or social housing agreement, co-ownership rules and local law permit them to let the other party occupy it on these terms — or they have written permission. **3.2 Accurate description.** The accommodation is as listed: photographs current and genuine, stated amenities present and working, and nothing undisclosed that would make a reasonable person decline — ongoing building work, a pending eviction, broken heating, or a housemate who hasn't agreed.

**3.3 Habitable and safe.** Fit to occupy, functioning locks, no known undisclosed safety hazard.

**3.4 Housemates and neighbours.** Where shared, every other occupant has agreed. Where the building or landlord requires notice of a guest, it has been given.

**3.5 Access.** Keys, codes or equivalent provided by the agreed start date and time, plus a reachable contact for the duration — including an alternative if they'll be unreachable.

**3.6 Utilities.** Electricity, water, heating and internet connected and paid for throughout, unless disclosed otherwise in writing beforehand.

## 4. What each party promises about the stay

Each party occupying accommodation agrees to:

- treat it and everything in it with reasonable care;
- follow any house rules given in writing before the stay;
- not exceed the agreed number of occupants, or let anyone else stay overnight, without prior written consent;
- not hold parties or events, cause nuisance to neighbours, or breach the other party's lease or building rules;
- not smoke or bring animals unless the listing permits it;
- not remove anything, or enter areas identified as private;
- not sublet or let anyone else occupy the accommodation;
- report any damage or problem **as soon as they notice it**, not at the end;
- leave on the agreed date and time, in a condition reasonably comparable to how they found it, allowing for ordinary use;
- return keys or access credentials as agreed.

## 5. Damage, loss and liability between the parties

**5.1** Each party is responsible to the other for damage they, or anyone they let in, cause beyond ordinary wear and tear.

**5.2** Neither is responsible for pre-existing damage or fair wear and tear.

**5.3 Evidence.** Both parties should take dated photographs of every room at the start and end of the stay and share them in the in-app chat. Without this, a later disagreement about damage is very hard to resolve.

**5.4 No deposit, no claims process.** Nothing secures these obligations, and **StudSwap has no claims process**. If someone causes damage and won't pay, the only routes are direct negotiation, insurance, or legal action between the two parties. If you'd like some protection against this, StudSwap's app offers an optional [Damage Deposit Agreement](/damage-deposit-agreement) template you and your match can use directly between yourselves; StudSwap still isn't a party to it.

**5.5 Insurance.** Neither party is insured by StudSwap. Contents and personal liability policies frequently exclude — or require notification of — a non-family occupant. **Check your own policy before confirming**, and consider whether you need to tell your insurer.

**5.6** Nothing here excludes liability for death or personal injury caused by a party's negligence.

## 6. Changes and cancellation

**6.1 Before both confirm.** Either may propose different dates or a different price. A new proposal replaces the old one and resets both confirmations. Nothing has been charged.

**6.2 After both confirm.** Either may cancel in the app. StudSwap's own consequences ([Terms of Service](/terms), Section 10):

| Notice before start | StudSwap's consequence |
|---|---|
| More than 7 days | €20 refunded to both. |
| 7 days or less | Canceller's €20 not refunded; the other party gets their €20 back plus €20 compensation from StudSwap. |

**6.3 Money already paid between the parties is not affected.** If the payer has already paid, **cancelling in the app does not reverse it and StudSwap cannot recover it.** The parties must sort it out between themselves. Where a stay is cancelled and has not happened, the receiving party should return what they were paid; if they don't, the payer's recourse is against them.

**6.4 The €20 is not a cap on liability.** It is a small fixed consequence StudSwap applies, not a settlement of all claims. If a late cancellation costs one party more — a non-refundable travel booking, say — they may still pursue the other for the difference. Equally, receiving €20 does not oblige anyone to treat it as full compensation.

**6.5 Circumstances outside either party's control.** If a stay becomes impossible for a reason genuinely outside either party's control — serious illness, bereavement, natural disaster, government restriction, or the accommodation becoming uninhabitable through no fault of the provider — the parties should agree to treat it as a no-fault cancellation and return any money paid.

`[DECISION] The app has no mutually-agreed no-fault cancellation flow. Without one, whoever clicks "cancel" takes the €20 hit regardless of the reason, which will produce unfair outcomes and support tickets in the first month. Consider a "cancel by mutual agreement" flow requiring both parties to confirm, refunding both €20.`

**6.6 Abandonment.** If a party who agreed to provide accommodation becomes uncontactable, or doesn't provide access at the agreed time, that counts as a cancellation by them at the moment access was due.

## 7. This is not a tenancy

The parties intend to create a short, personal, temporary permission to occupy — not a lease or tenancy — and no security of tenure is intended or granted. Neither party acquires any right to remain after the agreed end date.

`[LEGAL] ⚠️ Intention does not settle this. In several countries, exclusive occupation for a rent-like payment can create a protected tenancy whatever the parties call it, and once created it can be very hard to end. The risk is highest for One-Directional Stays, and the threshold differs across the EU, UK and Switzerland. A lawyer must advise what maximum duration keeps the arrangement safely outside tenancy protection in each country served — this feeds the maximum stay length in Section 14 of the Terms of Service.`
